# PROJECT LEADERSHIP — 6-HOUR PROJECT-READY COURSE
### Learn → Practice → Role-Play → Handle Pressure → Lead Real Projects
**Running case study: the "Analytics Modernization Program."**

> Keep three things in your pocket: **The 5 Questions** (What happened? Why? Impact? Options? Decision/action?), **The Executive Formula** (What? So what? Now what?), and **the core truth**: success is not finished tasks — it's a business outcome delivered through people.

## THE RUNNING PROJECT
Replace manual Excel reporting with automated Databricks pipelines + governed model + Power BI self-service dashboards. Fixed deadline, limited budget, changing requirements, data-quality problems, vendor dependency, security gates, many departments, high executive visibility. The *technology* you may know — the challenge is the *people*.

**Cast:** CEO (growth/reputation), CFO/Sponsor (cost/ROI/budget), CIO (secure/scalable), Business Head (faster decisions), BA (clear requirements), Data Eng Lead (clean pipelines), BI Lead (great dashboards), Analysts/Devs, Business Users (useful reports), Security (gatekeeper, slow), Finance, Operations, Vendor, PMO. Every concept maps onto these people.

# HOUR 1 — FUNDAMENTALS

**Value ladder:** TASK → DELIVERABLE → MILESTONE → OUTCOME → BUSINESS VALUE. (Lay bricks → finished wall → inspector sign-off → a family lives there → the family is safe. A leader never forgets the family.) Reporting "I finished my task" when nobody can get value yet is the junior trap.

**Management vs Leadership:** Management = Planning + Tracking + Coordination. Leadership = Management + Influence + Decisions + People + Alignment + Business thinking. You're hired and promoted on the leadership half.

**Mindset shift:** from "I completed my task" to "What does the project need next?" Growth path: TASK OWNER → WORKSTREAM OWNER → PROJECT OWNER → BUSINESS-OUTCOME OWNER.

**Ownership ≠ blame.** See problem → understand → take responsibility → coordinate → drive → communicate → follow through. *"I'll pick this up and come back by Thursday with options."*

**Success = Scope+Time+Cost+Quality+Risk+People+Stakeholders+Business Value.** You can finish every task and still fail.

**The 5 Questions** (use on every problem forever): What happened? (facts) · Why? (root cause) · Impact? (scope/time/cost/quality/outcome) · Options? (always >1) · Decision/action? (owner, by when).

**Charter** (birth certificate; most powerful field = OUT OF SCOPE): Problem · Business objective · Project objective · Outcome · In/Out scope · Deliverables · Timeline · Budget · Stakeholders · Risks · Dependencies · Success criteria · Sponsor · Lead.

**Scope creep** = death by a thousand reasonable "small" requests → route every change through change control (impact analysis → decision). **Iron triangle:** deadline fixed + scope up = reduce scope / add time / add resources / add budget / accept risk. Say the trade-off out loud; never "we'll try."

**✅ Checkpoint:** task/deliverable/milestone/outcome/value examples; management vs leadership in one line; an out-of-scope line that stops a fight; the 5 Questions on "a key analyst just resigned."

# HOUR 2 — STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT

A stakeholder = anyone who can influence / is affected by / decides about / cares about the project. If you only manage people who report to you, you're not leading a project — the ones who can *stop* you often don't.

**Find them:** who can STOP / APPROVE / FUND / USE / INFLUENCE / DELAY / is AFFECTED? The most dangerous is the one you didn't identify (Security, or the collective users).

**Power/Interest matrix:** Manage Closely (high/high) · Keep Satisfied (high power/low interest — don't surprise them) · Keep Informed (low power/high interest — allies, adoption) · Monitor (low/low). Positions move; re-map.

**Register:** Stakeholder · Role · Power · Interest · Influence · Concern · Expectation · Strategy.

**Motivation — beneath the words:** position → interest → fear. Solve interests, not positions.

**Expectation gap:** write it, read it back, re-confirm on every change. Unspoken expectations are landmines.

**Communication matrix:** right audience, right info, right cadence, right format. CEO=monthly 1-pager; Sponsor=weekly 1:1; Team=daily standup; Business=weekly workshop; Vendor=weekly review.

**Executive communication:** Status · Risk · Impact · Options · Decision — not every task/detail. **What? / So what? / Now what?** — "So what?" is the bridge from information to meaning that juniors skip.

**✅ Checkpoint:** place CEO/Security/user/vendor on the matrix; the hidden interest/fear behind "all 10 dashboards or none"; a 3-line What/So-what/Now-what for "finance data quality is worse than expected."

# HOUR 3 — PLANNING, GOVERNANCE & EXECUTION

**WBS:** PROJECT → PHASE → DELIVERABLE → WORKSTREAM → TASK → SUBTASK (one owner, estimable). **Milestones** = agreed "done" checkpoints; report milestones up, tasks down. **Dependencies** (internal/external/vendor/approval/technical/resource/data) — approval & vendor dependencies are the most underestimated. **Critical path** = longest dependent chain = delivery date; protect it, triage against it.

**RACI:** R (does), A (owns — exactly ONE), C (consulted), I (informed). Mistakes: no R (floats), two A's (nobody owns), everyone C (paralysis), everyone I (noise).

**RAID:** Risks (might) · Assumptions (believe, validate) · Issues (have) · Dependencies (waiting-ons).

**Risk vs Issue:** risk = mitigation + contingency; issue = root cause + owner + deadline. **Risk responses:** Avoid · Mitigate · Transfer · Accept (consciously). Score Likelihood×Impact; focus on High×High.

**Issue flow:** ISSUE → FACTS → IMPACT → ROOT CAUSE → OPTIONS → OWNER → DEADLINE → (escalate) → RESOLVE.

**Governance:** push decisions to the right level (team → lead → PMO → steering → sponsor → execs). **Decision log** beats "I never agreed to that."

**✅ Checkpoint:** draw the critical path + one off-path activity; risk or issue? ("Security hasn't approved, go-live next week"); one RACI row for "change a metric definition"; a mitigate + transfer response for "vendor may slip."

# HOUR 4 — DIFFICULT STAKEHOLDERS, CONFLICT & NEGOTIATION

**The 10 difficult stakeholders** (why → what not to do → what to do → phrase → escalation): angry, micromanager, unresponsive, unrealistic exec, constant changer, passive-aggressive, meeting dominator, technical blocker, blamer, overloaded member. (Full playbook in the stakeholder-management doc.)

**Framework:** DON'T REACT → UNDERSTAND → identify INTEREST → identify FEAR → identify POWER → identify CONSTRAINT → choose response. Difficult behavior is a symptom.

**Interest vs position (master key):** "I need it Friday" → "board reviews Monday" → maybe one chart by Thursday solves it. Ask *"what's driving that?"*

**Five conflict modes:** Avoid · Accommodate · Compete · Compromise · Collaborate — choose consciously; collaborate is the default for important things; compete sparingly.

**Conflict resolution:** FACTS → IMPACT → INTERESTS → OPTIONS → AGREEMENT → ACTION. Start with facts (cools the room).

**Difficult conversation:** "I want to make sure we're aligned." → AGREED → CHANGED → IMPACT → OPTIONS → RECOMMENDATION → AGREEMENT.

**Say no — A-C-I-O-R:** Acknowledge + Constraint + Impact + Options + Recommendation. No to the terms, not the person.

**Negotiation:** POSITION → INTEREST → CONSTRAINT → OPTIONS → TRADE-OFF → AGREEMENT.

**The 3-option move:** (A) keep scope → move date; (B) keep date → reduce scope; (C) keep both → add resources (+risk). Converts a demand into a decision they own.

**Escalation (responsible, not failure):** Issue + Facts + Impact + Actions taken + Options + Recommendation + Decision needed + Deadline. Don't escalate things within your authority or first-time hiccups you haven't tried to solve.

**✅ Checkpoint:** pro "no" to "add 3 reports by Friday"; a dominator hijacking your workshop; a full escalation for "Security won't approve before go-live week."

# HOUR 5 — LEADERSHIP, COMMUNICATION & EXECUTIVE MANAGEMENT

**Leading without authority:** Authority = must obey; Influence = want to follow. You lead by influence. Model: CREDIBILITY + RELATIONSHIP + CLARITY + DATA + TRUST + CONSISTENCY.

**Trust:** do what you say; say what you know; admit what you don't; raise risks early; don't hide bad news; give credit; protect the team; follow through. Built in drops, lost in buckets.

**Communicating bad news:** Bad news + Early + Impact + Options + Recommendation = leadership. Being first to raise it makes you look *more* in control.

**Translate per audience:** devs (technical clarity), users (value), managers (progress+risk), execs (outcome+decision), vendors (commitment+accountability).

**Team:** burnout/overload — renegotiate scope or date before you break people; underperformers — address privately, early, diagnose cause; high performers — growth/recognition; unclear ownership — RACI + "who owns this?". **1:1s:** how's it going? what's blocking you? what do you need from me? what risks? what should we change? — catch burnout/risks before the status report.

**Meetings:** right type for the need; every meeting = Purpose + Agenda + Right people + Decision + Time + Owner + Next steps. Facilitation: start on time, time-box, draw in the quiet, redirect dominators, read back decisions, every action = owner + date.

**Decisions:** FACTS → CONSTRAINTS → OPTIONS → TRADE-OFFS → RISK → RECOMMENDATION → DECISION; log them.

**Change management (adoption):** CURRENT → WHY → FUTURE → IMPACT → RESISTANCE → COMMS → TRAINING → ADOPTION. Reading resistance: don't understand→explain; don't agree→discuss; don't know how→train; don't want to→what's in it for them; don't trust→proof+involve; this hurts me→address the fear.

**Recovery:** STOP → ASSESS → GET FACTS → CRITICAL PATH → PRIORITIZE → FREEZE SCOPE → RESOLVE DEPENDENCIES → REALLOCATE → RECOVERY PLAN → ALIGN → EXECUTE → MONITOR.

**✅ Checkpoint:** translate "schema drift in the pipeline" for CEO and user; an engineer missing deadlines — which resistance type & how to check; first three moves inheriting a project 3 weeks behind.

# HOUR 6 — COMPLETE SIMULATION (CAPSTONE)

**Week 12 of 20:** requirements +25%, vendor 2 wks late, poor data quality, security delayed, users disagree, BI overloaded, CEO won't move the date, CFO wants more, budget frozen.

**Stakeholder map (model):** CEO manage-closely/crisp options; CFO ally/3 options; CIO unblock security; Security fast-track via CIO; Business Head facilitate prioritization; Vendor recovery plan + accountability; BI Lead protect/freeze scope; Users prioritization workshop.

**Health (model, defend each):** Scope🔴 (+25%, no freeze) · Schedule🔴 (vendor on critical path) · Cost🟠 · Quality🔴 (finance data) · Risk🔴 · Resources🟠 · Stakeholders🟠 · Dependencies🔴 → **RED, recoverable.**

**Recovery:** freeze scope at the agreed top-10 · parallelize testing + security via CIO · reallocate one engineer (no new budget) · fast-follow the +25% and CFO extras · sponsor decision by Friday.

**Role-play models:**
- **CEO** ("why isn't it ready?") → lead with outcome: on track for the 10 dashboards that drive your decisions; two threats in hand; to protect the date with no new budget — freeze scope, parallelize testing+security, move one engineer, fast-follow extras; need one decision Friday.
- **CFO** ("3 more reports") → 3 options (A add & slip, B swap for 3 lower, C on-time + fast-follow in 2 weeks); recommend C.
- **Tech Lead** ("requirements keep changing") → empathize, scope frozen today, change control on, agree top-2, remove one blocker now.
- **Angry user** ("useless") → investigate first ("what decision doesn't it support?"), find requirement/expectation/comms gap, return with a fix by a date.
- **Vendor** ("2 more weeks") → demand a recovery plan: add resource, parallelize, revised milestones, checkpoints every 2 days, in writing.

**Steering committee:** Status(RAG) → top-3 Risks/Issues → Options(A/B/C) → Recommendation → DRIVE the decision → owners → dates → close.

**Objective RAG criteria:** 🟢 ≤5% variance, no action · 🟠 5–15%, recovery plan in place · 🔴 >15% or a blocking issue. **Never hide a red project behind green reporting** (watermelon projects always burst).

# THE FINAL MENTAL MODEL
When someone says "we have a problem with the project," you automatically run: What happened? → Business impact? → What's at risk? → Who's affected? → Who owns the decision? → Options? → Trade-offs? → Recommendation? → Now what? → Owner? → By when? → How do we communicate? That reflex is what makes you project-ready.
